1. Principal-agent problem in a developing country
2. Land misallocation
3. Air protection, land protection



This study contributes to three distinct strands of literature. First and foremost, this paper fills in the research gap on the environmental costs of land regulation in the rural region in a developing country. Existing empirical literature extensively examines the impacts of land regulation on housing prices (Hilber and Vermeulen, 2016), economic growth (Besley and Burgess, 2000), and productivity (Beg, 2022; Adamopoulos et al., 2022). However, these studies predominantly address urban contexts and largely overlook environmental consequences. Among the studies set against a Chinese backdrop, most focus on urban areas such as Henderson et al. (2022) and Cai et al. (2017). Rural-centric studies (Adamopoulos, 2022; Cheng and Chung, 2018) tend to concentrate on property rights and resource misallocation. This paper uniquely contributes by investigating the environmental costs induced by land regulation in rural areas.

Secondly, this paper relates to the literature on principal-agent problems within centralized governmental structures (Nove and Nove, 1969; Kornai, 1959) and the contract theory literature on multitasking (Holmstrom and Milgrom 1991; Hart, Shleifer, and Vishny 1997). By evaluating the cropland quality gap under various regulatory burdens, this study provides novel evidence of misalignment in local policy implementation due to imperfect performance monitoring. It also explores the decision-making dilemmas faced by local bureaucrats, who must balance economic growth with the enforcement of land regulations (Fan et al., 2023; Chen et al., 2018). This research illuminates how local-level regulatory enforcement may be skewed to favor economic objectives, thereby contributing to the broader discourse on governance and regulatory efficacy.

Lastly, while there is growing attention to the environmental repercussions of economic growth, much of the existing research has concentrated on air and water pollution (Buntaine et al., 2024; Axbard and Deng, 2024; He et al., 2020). This study diverges by focusing on the less-studied impact of land misallocation, particularly its role in increasing geological disasters under stringent land regulation frameworks.
